What the Bus Line Refresh does to your block

A pretend article, kept in the repo at docs/embed-example.html, to show the map behaving as somebody else's page furniture. The prose is filler; the three maps are the real thing, live from prt-refresh.lemaliconsulting.com. Open this file straight in a browser — nothing serves it.

1. Dropped in as-is

No parameters beyond the mode. The reader gets the county-wide Locations map, the toolbar, and the key — which is also the summary sentence for whatever is on screen. Try the wheel over it: it scrolls this page rather than zooming the map. Ctrl+wheel, the zoom buttons, or two fingers work the map.

2. Opened at the question the article is about

An article about losing the one-seat ride to Oakland should not open on the county default and ask the reader to find it. Every control is a URL parameter, so the embed opens already answering — here, one-seat rides to Oakland at the strict 150 m walk, on a Saturday, centred on the South Hills. 

?embed=1
&view=oneseat          dots | surface | both | corridors | oneseat | journey
&dest=oakland          downtown | oakland | lat,lon
&radius=150            400 (quarter mile) | 150 (same corner)
&oneseatday=selected   any (published) | selected
&day=saturday          weekday | saturday | sunday
&map=40.4100,-80.0050,12.30    where the map is looking
&at=40.4406,-79.9959          ask at a point (optional)

3. In a narrow column

At a few hundred pixels the map keeps working: the toolbar folds into the button at top left, the key folds to its head line, and the zoom buttons stay, since there is no pinch on a desktop and the wheel now belongs to this page.

Filler column, so the map beside it is in a realistic sidebar rather than alone on a white page. The street view is the one that reads best small: red is pavement that loses its only bus, blue is pavement that gains one.

Street mileage is not access — a street can lose its only bus while a parallel block a minute's walk away keeps one. Quote it beside the coverage figures, never alone.

What an embed gives up

The answer panel. In the full site a click opens a column of figures — buses per day before and after, by day type, with the routes involved. An embed has no room for it, so a click still asks (the walk circle and the recoloured dots are the map's own answer) and the corner link carries the reader to the full site at that exact point.

That link is why it should not be styled away: on an embedded map it is the only thing saying whose map this is, and the only route to the method and the caveats — which have to travel with any number from this project.

One thing worth a line in your own privacy notice: the map loads its background tiles from OpenFreeMap, so your readers' browsers reach a third party from inside your page.
