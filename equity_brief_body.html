Who gains and who loses under the Bus Line Refresh

Coverage at every populated census block in Allegheny County, weighted by who lives there. Read across all six equity questions at once, the plan's trade runs progressive — car-free, low-income and Black residents come out ahead of the county on nearly every tier. The exception is age.

Losing more is not the same as losing ground

Each dot is one group on one tier, placed by how fast it loses service compared with the county as a whole (across) and how fast it gains service back (up). Dot size is the group's population.

Only the shaded corner is losing ground: above the county rate on losses and below it on gains. Five groups lose net access to any bus at all, particularly residents aged 65 and over.

Where the trade landed

The same result in each group's own percentage points. Every group loses some plain coverage and every group gains weekend frequency; what differs is the exchange rate. Read the two columns together — sorted by the left one, the right one is close to its mirror image.

Where this happened

The losses are concentrated. Of Allegheny County's 1,062 census block groups, 182 lose coverage and 75 gain it; a hundred of them hold 90% of the loss. So this is a list rather than a map — and the list says something the county totals do not: the coverage being trimmed is suburban. Baldwin, Ross, McCandless, Mount Lebanon, Kennedy, Scott, Plum, Bethel Park. That is the same geography behind both the age finding and the white-residents finding above; they are one result seen through two variables, not two independent results.

Car-free households are the ones with no fallback, so they are the first table.

And the plan does add buses to ground that has none today — most of all in McKeesport, and in West View, Brackenridge and Robinson. The fourth row is unnamed because it takes its name from the nearest bus stop and there isn't one: four block groups on Perry Highway around Wexford, where nothing stops within 3 km today and the plan puts ten stops, including one at AHN Wexford. They have no name to borrow for the same reason they are gaining a bus.

What this does not say

This describes places, not people. It measures the neighbourhoods a group lives in, not its members — a census block group is covered or not, and everyone in it is counted the same way. It cannot tell you that any particular person gained or lost a bus.

Population is one of three denominators, and it is not the whole answer. By location the plan is roughly service-neutral; by area it covers about 12% less ground. Quote this page beside those, never instead of them.

Disability and language are tract-level estimates shared down to block groups by population, so they assume a uniform rate across a whole tract. Treat them as indicative.

Walking 400 m is not the same everywhere. Grade, sidewalks, crossings and shelters decide whether a stop is usable, and none of them are measured here.
